# Has the Literature Already Answered "Does State-Correlation of the Observation Process Determine When Inference Beats Memory in Deep-RL POMDPs"? A Novelty Audit

## TL;DR
- **Verdict: [clean gap], moderate-to-high confidence.** After a systematic search, no paper was found that uses the *structure* of observation corruption — state-correlated/heteroscedastic vs. state-independent/homoscedastic — as the explicit experimental knob governing the empirical advantage of explicit latent-state inference (world models / belief methods) over a strong recurrent memory baseline (LSTM/GRU PPO/SAC) in deep-RL POMDPs. The specific causal characterization in the hypothesis is unclaimed.
- **The classical "inference dominates memory" result is real but does NOT pre-empt the claim.** Belief-state sufficiency (Åström 1965; Smallwood & Sondik 1973; Kaelbling, Littman & Cassandra 1998) settles the theory in the idealized, model-known case. It says nothing about the *empirical* deep-RL regime with function approximation, and nothing about observation-state coupling as the determining variable.
- **The contribution must be framed narrowly to survive.** The defensible novelty is the empirical deep-RL characterization tying the inference-vs-memory gap to state-correlation of the observation channel. Adjacent results — inference advantage growing with noise *magnitude* (DVRL), and state-dependent-missingness modeling helping most when missingness is informative (MNAR/HMM statistics) — establish the plausibility of the mechanism but do not occupy the claim.

## Resolution of the Critical Nuance
**What the literature HAS established (classical theory).** In a POMDP with a known model, the belief state b_t = P(s_t | h_t) is a sufficient statistic for the interaction history, and the optimal policy can condition on b_t rather than on raw history; this is the foundational result attributed to Åström (1965) and developed by Smallwood & Sondik (1973) and Kaelbling, Littman & Cassandra (1998, *Artificial Intelligence* 101:99–134). In this idealized sense, "inference dominates finite-window memory" is textbook. This makes the *trivial* version of the question answered.

**What the literature HAS NOT established (empirical deep-RL characterization tied to state-correlation).** In deep RL with function approximation, recurrent policies are themselves *approximating* belief states by backpropagating policy loss through an RNN, so the theoretical dominance does not transfer cleanly. Two empirical sub-questions remain open: (1) *when* does an explicit-inference / world-model approach beat a well-tuned recurrent baseline; and (2) is state-correlation of the observation process the knob that governs that gap? The literature has partial answers to (1) — inference tends to help more as noise *magnitude* rises (DVRL) and on "noisy" vs. merely "partial" tasks (Malekzadeh & Plataniotis) — but has not isolated *the structure* of the corruption (state-dependent vs. state-independent) as the governing factor. Question (2) is unclaimed in deep RL.

## Key Findings
1. **The strong-recurrent-baseline result is the central obstacle, and it is well established.** Ni, Eysenbach & Salakhutdinov ("Recurrent Model-Free RL Can Be a Strong Baseline for Many POMDPs," ICML 2022, arXiv 2110.05038) compare a well-tuned recurrent off-policy agent against 21 environments drawn from 6 prior specialized POMDP methods (spanning meta-RL, robust RL, and temporal credit assignment), and report that their recurrent agent "achieves greater sample efficiency and asymptotic performance than these methods on 18/21 environments." This is exactly the baseline the thesis must beat, and it means "inference helps" cannot be assumed — it must be demonstrated against a tuned recurrent policy.

2. **Inference advantage growing with noise MAGNITUDE is already shown — but magnitude, not structure.** Igl, Zintgraf, Le, Wood & Whiteson ("Deep Variational Reinforcement Learning for POMDPs," ICML 2018, arXiv 1806.02426) report that DVRL's "performance deteriorates more slowly for increasing observation noise, showing the advantage of DVRL's inference computations in encoding the history in the presence of observation noise." Critically, the noise is additive Gaussian on the Mountain Hike map and they sweep its *level* (σ_o), not its *state-correlation structure*. This is the canonical "inference helps more under noise" result, and it concerns magnitude only.

3. **The closest inference-vs-recurrent comparison makes only a qualitative "gap grows under noise" remark.** Malekzadeh & Plataniotis ("Active Inference and Reinforcement Learning," arXiv 2212.07946) compare belief-inference methods (their G-SAC/G-Dreamer, plus Dreamer, VRM) against a Recurrent Model-Free baseline and find the inference methods consistently win, with the gap "more pronounced in the case of noisy observations." Crucially, this is a contrast between *separate fixed task variants* ("noisy" vs. "partial") with fixed additive noise — not a controlled sweep of noise structure — mirroring DVRL's own setup of additive Gaussian noise at fixed/varied magnitude. State-correlation is never named as the determining factor.

4. **State-disentanglement world-model work varies distractor variability/SNR, not state-correlation, and has no recurrent baseline.** Cao et al. ("Rethinking State Disentanglement in Causal Reinforcement Learning," arXiv 2408.13498, AAAI 2025) manipulate pixel-distractor conditions (noiseless / uniform / diverse background) and assume additive, largely state-independent noise; all baselines are RSSM world models, so it does not test the inference-vs-memory gap and does not use state-correlation as a knob.

5. **The exact statistical phenomenon IS documented — but outside deep RL.** In missing-data and HMM statistics, modeling state-dependent (informative / MNAR) missingness helps most precisely when missingness is informative, and degrades gracefully to no harm when data are MAR/MCAR — the same "helps under state-dependence, harmless under state-independence" pattern the thesis posits. This appears in MNAR time-series imputation work and in state-dependent-missingness HMM models, but in latent dynamical-system / clinical inference settings, not deep-RL control with a recurrent baseline.

6. **State-dependent observation/measurement models are studied in estimation and control, but disconnected from the RL inference-vs-memory question.** The Kalman-filtering literature treats state-dependent (heteroscedastic/multiplicative) measurement noise (e.g., generalized EKF work; belief-space control under state-dependent measurement noise, arXiv 2510.14100), showing filters that model the state-dependence outperform those that ignore it. This corroborates the mechanism but addresses estimation accuracy, not the RL policy-performance gap between inference and memory.

7. **State-dependent observation structure also appears, named differently, in the ACNO-MDP and underreporting-surveillance literatures — but again not as the inference-vs-memory knob.** In Action-Contingent Noiselessly Observable MDPs (Nam, Fleming & Brunskill, NeurIPS 2021), the observation space is constrained to O = S ∪ {missingness}, where observing returns the true state (p(o|s′, a_observe)=1 iff o=s′) and not observing always returns an uninformative "missingness" symbol — motivated by healthcare settings balancing test information value against test cost. Separately, disease-surveillance is a real-world instance of state-correlated corruption: reporting completeness is both low and state-coupled — a US review (Doyle et al., 1970–1999) found infectious-disease reporting completeness ranged from 9% to 99% and was strongly associated with the disease being reported, and CDC's *Principles of Epidemiology* cites multiplication factors up to 20–38× for under-reported enteric pathogens. None of this work ties policy performance to state-correlation of the corruption or compares inference against a tuned recurrent baseline along that axis.

## Adjacent Literatures Checked
- **Ecology: imperfect detection, occupancy & N-mixture models.** *Ruled adjacent (context), not a hit.* N-mixture models (J. Andrew Royle, "N-mixture models for estimating population size from spatially replicated counts," *Biometrics* 60(1):108–115, 2004) explicitly model detection probability p in y|N ~ Binomial(N, p) and accommodate detection covarying with abundance — a clean statistical instance of state-correlated observation. But this literature is about *estimating* abundance, is not sequential decision-making, and has essentially no connection to RL/control policy comparison. It is excellent motivating/analogy material, not prior art.
- **MNAR / informative missingness.** *Ruled adjacent, but the most conceptually proximate.* The Rubin taxonomy (MCAR/MAR/MNAR) and informative-missingness work formalize exactly the state-independent vs. state-correlated distinction. Recent MNAR time-series and HMM-with-state-dependent-missingness papers demonstrate the precise "advantage grows with informative/state-dependent missingness, harmless otherwise" pattern. This is the strongest evidence the mechanism is real — and the strongest terminology-boundary risk (see "What would change this verdict"). But these are imputation/latent-dynamics inference papers, not deep-RL control with a recurrent baseline.
- **Control/estimation under state-dependent noise.** *Ruled adjacent.* Heteroscedastic/multiplicative measurement-noise filtering (generalized EKF, belief-space safety-critical control) confirms that modeling state-dependence improves estimation, but does not address the RL inference-vs-memory policy gap.
- **Distractor / background-noise model-based RL.** *Ruled adjacent.* Work on noisy-background DMC and reconstruction-free world models studies robustness to *task-irrelevant* distractors, not state-correlated corruption of the task-relevant signal, and typically compares world models to each other.

## Assessment
**The gap is real, and it is narrow but genuine.** The thesis is not pre-empted: no paper combines (a) deep-RL POMDPs, (b) an explicit-inference-vs-tuned-recurrent-memory comparison, and (c) state-correlation of the observation process as the experimental variable that governs the gap. However, the surrounding territory is well-populated, so the contribution cannot be stated broadly. The most defensible novelty claim is:

> *"In deep-RL POMDPs, the empirical advantage of explicit latent-state inference over a strong recurrent memory baseline is governed by the state-correlation structure of the observation process: under state-independent (homoscedastic, fixed-emission) corruption recurrent policies degrade gracefully, whereas under state-correlated corruption (heteroscedastic noise or informative/state-dependent undercounting that biases the highest-state regimes) recurrent policies fail systematically and explicit inference recovers the lost performance. To our knowledge this is the first work to isolate observation-state coupling — rather than noise magnitude or partial-vs-noisy task type — as the determining property."*

This phrasing explicitly distinguishes the claim from DVRL (magnitude), Malekzadeh & Plataniotis (noisy-vs-partial task type), and the classical sufficiency theorem.

## Recommendations
1. **Proceed, but pre-commit to the narrow framing above.** Stake the contribution on *observation-state coupling as the governing knob*, not on "inference beats memory" (pre-empted by theory) or "inference helps under noise" (pre-empted by DVRL/Malekzadeh & Plataniotis). Benchmark threshold: if a reviewer can restate your result as "more noise → inference helps more," the framing has failed and must be sharpened back to structure-vs-magnitude.
2. **Design the experiment as an explicit 2-way manipulation.** Hold noise *magnitude* (e.g., marginal observation entropy or expected distortion) constant while toggling *structure* (homoscedastic additive vs. state-correlated heteroscedastic/informative-missingness). This is the single design that no prior paper has run and is the crux of the novelty. Without holding magnitude fixed, the result collapses into the DVRL finding.
3. **Use Ni et al. (2022) recurrent model-free RL as the primary memory baseline**, tuned per their published recipe, against Dreamer/DVRL/SLAC-style inference. Beating a *weak* recurrent baseline would be dismissed; the strong baseline is the point.
4. **Cite and explicitly distance from the three near/adjacent anchors** (DVRL 1806.02426, Active Inference & RL 2212.07946, Cao et al. 2408.13498) in the related-work section, stating for each precisely what axis they varied (magnitude; noisy-vs-partial task type; distractor variability/SNR) versus your axis (state-correlation).
5. **Import the MNAR/HMM result as theoretical scaffolding, not as a competitor.** Framing your RL finding as the control-policy analogue of the established "informative-missingness modeling helps iff missingness is informative" statistical result both strengthens plausibility and pre-empts the "isn't this obvious?" critique by showing the deep-RL version is genuinely untested.
6. **Run one MNAR-in-RL terminology sweep before submission** (see below) to retire the largest residual pre-emption risk.

## What Would Change This Verdict
- **A deep-RL paper that sweeps observation-noise STRUCTURE.** If any paper varied heteroscedastic vs. homoscedastic (or informative- vs. uninformative-missingness) observation noise as an experimental axis and reported the inference-vs-recurrent gap as a function of it, the claim would be pre-empted. None was found; this is the highest-risk scenario.
- **A robustness/representation paper that names state-dependence as the determining factor.** A paper concluding that the *coupling* between latent state and observation corruption (not its level) is what makes recurrent policies fail would narrow the claim to a replication/extension.
- **An MNAR-in-RL bridge.** A paper porting informative-missingness theory into deep-RL POMDP control with a recurrent baseline (the MNAR/HMM statistics field is one citation away from this) would substantially narrow novelty. This is the most likely place a pre-empting paper could emerge, and the targeted search to run is "MNAR / informative missingness + reinforcement learning + belief/world model + recurrent baseline."

## Caveats
- This is a negative result established by systematic search; *one cannot prove a paper does not exist.* All conclusions are stated "to our knowledge after systematic search."
- **Terminology-boundary risk is the dominant threat.** The phenomenon travels under many names — heteroscedastic/multiplicative/signal-dependent observation noise, informative missingness, MNAR, imperfect detection, state-dependent emission/measurement error. A relevant deep-RL paper could exist under vocabulary not searched; the MNAR-in-RL boundary is especially live.
- The two candidate papers most central to the verdict (arXiv 2212.07946 and 2408.13498) were verified at the level of their experimental design and claims; the broader corpus was assessed via abstracts and targeted full-text reads, so a buried experiment in an appendix could exist.
- Several 2026-dated arXiv preprints surfaced during the search; preprints are not peer-reviewed and their claims should be treated as provisional. (Some surfaced items — e.g., an HMM-POMDP Formula-1 strategy preprint — were explicitly pre-registration documents whose empirical validation had not yet occurred.)
- The search prioritized arXiv/OpenReview/major-venue ML literature and statistical missing-data sources; it did not exhaustively cover domain silos (e.g., quantitative epidemiology, econometrics of measurement error) where the same mechanism may be formalized without RL framing.
